# drClckd: the Clocked arrangement

drClckd is Impromptu Modular's **Clocked** (GPL-3.0), and its panel now follows Clocked's own
arrangement rather than a compression of it. What follows is the reading of the original that the
layout is built from, and every place we depart from it.

## What the original does

Clocked is 16 HP and reads in three horizontal bands with no rules between them. Grouping is done by
**colour and by tray**, not by dividers.

**The top two rows are the master, and they are a grid.** The upper row is the three inputs — reset,
run, BPM — then the tempo knob, then the tempo display. The lower row is what each of those inputs
also has as a panel control: a reset button under the reset jack, a run button under the run jack,
the BPM mode buttons under the BPM jack. A column means "these are the same thing, by hand and by
cable". The master's own swing and pulse width finish that row.

**The three clock rows are identical and self-contained.** Each is: a large ratio knob, a dash, the
ratio display, a blinking lamp, then that clock's swing, pulse width and delay. Everything about
clock 2 is in the row that says 2. The labels repeat on every row — SWING 1, SWING 2, SWING 3 — so a
row can be read without counting down from the top.

**Colour codes function, across all four rows.** Blue is time — the tempo knob and the three ratios.
Orange is swing. Green is pulse width. Purple is delay. You can find every swing on the panel without
reading a word.

**The outputs sit in trays.** Two grey rounded wells along the bottom: the transport out (reset, run,
BPM) on the left, the three clocks on the right. The master clock output is alone in a third tray at
the top right, beside the display — the one output you patch first, nearest the tempo you set it by.

## What we take, exactly

- The **two-row master grid**, jacks over their buttons.
- The **three self-contained clock rows**, ratio knob and display at the left, labels repeated.
- The **colour code**: blue time, orange swing, green pulse width, purple delay.
- **16 HP**, which is what the arrangement costs. Ours was 14 and reached it by taking things away.
- **No rules between the bands.** Colour and column do the grouping, as they do in the original.

## Where we depart, and why

**The ratio is a knob and a display again.** It had become a scrolled readout — one control instead
of two, which was the right trade when the panel was 14 HP and out of room. At 16 it is not, and the
original is right: a detented knob is a better way to land on ×4 than a wheel, and the display then
has one job.

**The BPM mode is a radio, not two buttons.** The original walks the BPM input's meaning through
seven states with a `- MODE +` pair and one yellow LED, because it has one display and everything
queues for it. Ours has two states, CV and clock, and both are on the panel where you can read them.

**PPQN is on the panel.** In the original it is inside the mode paging. Here it is a small knob with
its value printed above it in white — the house's printed-value control.

**All seven outputs stay along the bottom**, in two groups with a gap, rather than three trays with
the master clock at the top right. Outputs along the bottom edge is a rack-wide convention here and
a module that broke it would cost more in the patch than it gained on the panel. The gap does the
grouping the trays did.

**No grey trays.** We have no well primitive, and the gap plus the OUT rule already say what the tray
said.
